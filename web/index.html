The market

How the IEX Day-Ahead Market works

Power for tomorrow is bought and sold today, in an auction on the Indian Energy Exchange. The copilot's job is to bid in that auction for the battery. Here is how the auction works, with real numbers.

Today · 10:00 – 12:00

1. Bids go in

Buyers (state power companies, big factories) and sellers (power plants, batteries) bid for each of tomorrow's 96 fifteen-minute slots, for example "buy 2.5 MWh in the 13:00–13:15 slot at up to ₹3,000".

This is when the copilot submits the battery's orders.

Today · about 13:00

2. Prices are set

For each slot, the exchange lines up buy bids from the highest price down and sell bids from the lowest price up. Where the two lines cross is that slot's clearing price. Everyone matched pays or receives that same price.

Tomorrow · 00:00 – 24:00

3. Power flows

The result is binding. The battery charges and discharges exactly as matched, and is paid at the clearing price.

The catch: step 1 happens before step 2, so the copilot must guess tomorrow's prices.

So the problem, and our answer
  1. Problem Bids for tomorrow go in today, knowing only prices up to today.
  2. Step 1 Forecast all 96 of tomorrow's clearing prices.
  3. Step 2 An optimiser picks the cheapest slots to buy in and the dearest to sell in, within the battery's limits.
  4. Step 3 Those become the orders submitted between 10:00 and 12:00.

Curve shapes are illustrative. Each slot's total buy bids, total sell bids, cleared volume and clearing price are real IEX Day-Ahead Market figures.
