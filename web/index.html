How it works

Three simple steps, built on serious maths

Each morning the copilot does a trader's homework in under a second.

1

Guess tomorrow's prices

Two models make a guess for each of the 96 slots, and we blend them: LEAR, the standard research model for electricity prices (Lago et al., 2021), and Chronos, a deep-learning foundation model from Amazon trained on millions of time series (Ansari et al., 2024). Chronos also gives a likely range.

2

Find the best plan

An optimiser tries every possible way to fill and empty the battery and keeps the one that earns the most, after losses and wear. It never breaks the battery's limits. On this page it runs live in your browser (dynamic programming); the reported results use the exact linear-programming version.

3

Send the orders

The plan becomes buy and sell orders for each 15-minute slot, with safety price limits from the likely range, so a surprise price cannot hurt much.

Why blend two models?

Being closer on price is not the same as earning more. Chronos misses prices about as much as copying yesterday, yet earns more because it gets the shape of the day right. The blend earns the most.

How we know it is not luck

The maths, in one line

Maximise  Σ price × sold − price × bought − wear × sold  over the 96 slots, while stored energy = yesterday's energy + 94% of what is bought − what is sold ÷ 94%, stored energy stays between 10% and 100%, and the battery never moves more than its power limit in 15 minutes.
